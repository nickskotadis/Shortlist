Frontend Engineer, Mobile Web
Kestrel Transit — Chicago, IL (Hybrid)

Kestrel builds the rider-facing apps for regional transit agencies. Eleven agencies use our white-label platform, covering about 3 million monthly riders. Most of those riders reach us through mobile web, not the native app, because they are on a bus and they need arrival times right now and they are not going to install anything.

We are hiring a Frontend Engineer focused on the mobile web experience.

Context you should have

Our mobile web app is a PWA. It has to work on a two-bar LTE connection in a subway tunnel, on a five-year-old Android device, for a rider who has ten seconds of attention. Every technical decision runs through that filter.

We are also a vendor to public agencies, which means long procurement cycles, contractual uptime commitments, and accessibility requirements that are legally enforceable rather than aspirational.

Responsibilities

Own the mobile web client (React, TypeScript, Workbox for service worker management, MapLibre for mapping).

Improve offline and degraded-network behavior. Today we cache schedule data reasonably well and real-time arrival data not at all, which is the wrong way round for what riders actually need.

Work on the map surface. Rendering a live vehicle layer over a transit network on a low-end phone without draining the battery is the single hardest technical problem on this team.

Support agency-specific theming. Each of our eleven agencies has its own branding, and a few have accessibility requirements stricter than the baseline.

Contribute to our performance budget work. Our target is interactive in under 3 seconds on a simulated Moto G4 over regular 3G, and we currently miss it on four of eleven agency configurations.

Requirements

Three or more years of frontend engineering with real mobile web experience — not just responsive layouts, but actual work targeting constrained devices and networks.

Strong React and TypeScript.

Experience with service workers and offline-first patterns.

Understanding of performance measurement on mobile: Lighthouse, WebPageTest, real device testing. We keep a drawer of old phones and you will use them.

Comfort with the constraints of white-label multi-tenancy.

Nice to have

Mapping or geospatial experience (MapLibre, Mapbox GL, Leaflet, or equivalent).

Experience with GTFS or transit data formats. We will teach you this if you have not seen it.

Public-sector or civic tech background.

Details

$130,000 - $160,000 plus benefits. Hybrid in Chicago, two days a week in the Loop office. Public transit stipend, which we would feel silly not offering.

Kestrel is 75 people. About a third of us came from transit agencies rather than from tech, which makes for unusually good product instincts and unusually long meetings.
