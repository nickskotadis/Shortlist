COMPANY AND DIVISION

Halbrook Technologies is a professional services and software firm serving federal civilian agencies, the Department of Defense, and state government customers. The company employs approximately 5,800 people, of whom roughly 2,100 hold active security clearances.

The Federal and State Government Solutions Division builds and operates mission systems under contract. Our portfolio includes benefits eligibility platforms for three state agencies, a case management system supporting a federal civilian agency, and several classified programs about which this posting can say nothing further.

The Security Engineering group within FSGS is responsible for the security architecture, controls implementation, continuous monitoring, and authorization support across this portfolio. We are a team of 26 across three sub-groups: application security, cloud and infrastructure security, and governance, risk, and compliance.

ROLE SUMMARY

We are hiring a Staff Security Engineer to serve as the senior technical authority for cloud and application security across the division's programs.

This is a hands-on senior individual contributor role. The position carries no direct reports but does carry substantial technical authority, including the ability to block a release or a deployment on security grounds and the responsibility to justify that decision to program leadership when it happens.

The role exists because our portfolio has grown faster than our senior security capacity, and because the authorization environment has become materially more demanding. Several of our programs are transitioning from legacy Authority to Operate processes toward continuous authorization models, and that transition requires engineering capability we currently have to borrow from programs rather than provide centrally.

PRINCIPAL DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Security Architecture and Engineering

Serve as the security architect for cloud environments across the portfolio, principally AWS GovCloud and Azure Government, including boundary design, network segmentation, identity architecture, encryption strategy, and logging architecture.

Design and implement security controls satisfying applicable frameworks, principally NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5 at the Moderate and High baselines, with FedRAMP requirements applying to certain systems and CMMC requirements applying to others.

Lead threat modeling for major systems and significant architectural changes. Produce documented threat models that inform design decisions rather than being generated after the fact for audit purposes.

Design and implement automated security control validation. A central objective for this role is moving our control evidence from manual periodic collection to continuous automated collection, which is both a compliance improvement and a substantial reduction in the burden currently carried by engineering teams.

Own the security architecture review process for new systems and major changes, including the documentation that supports authorization packages.

Application Security

Conduct and lead secure code review for high-risk components across programs. Our portfolio includes systems written in Java, C#, Python, and JavaScript, with a small amount of C in one legacy component.

Own static analysis, dynamic analysis, and software composition analysis tooling across the division, including tuning to reduce false positive burden. Current tooling produces a volume of findings that engineering teams have substantially learned to disregard, which is the failure mode this role should correct.

Manage the vulnerability lifecycle: identification, triage, risk assessment, remediation tracking, and reporting. Certain findings carry contractually mandated remediation timelines and missing them has consequences at the contract level.

Support penetration testing, including scoping, coordination with independent assessors, and remediation of findings. Several programs require annual independent assessment.

Develop secure coding standards and reference implementations, and deliver training to engineering staff across programs.

Authorization and Compliance Support

Support Authority to Operate packages and continuous authorization activities, working with our GRC sub-group, with program Information System Security Officers, and with government Authorizing Officials and their representatives.

Prepare and present technical security material to government stakeholders, including during assessment activities. This requires the ability to explain a control implementation precisely and to acknowledge a gap without becoming defensive, which is a specific skill and not everyone has it.

Support incident response for security events affecting program systems, including the reporting obligations that attach to incidents in government environments, which are time-bound and unforgiving.

Contribute to proposal efforts by providing security architecture content and by assessing the security implications of proposed technical approaches.

CURRENT TECHNICAL LANDSCAPE

Cloud environments across the portfolio are principally AWS GovCloud, with two programs in Azure Government and one legacy system remaining in a government-furnished data center. Infrastructure is managed through Terraform with an internal module library, though adoption is uneven and two programs still provision substantially by hand.

Container workloads run on Amazon EKS and on a hardened Kubernetes distribution in one classified environment. Container image scanning is in place; runtime security monitoring is not, and establishing it is an identified gap.

Identity is federated through a combination of agency-provided identity providers, PIV and CAC authentication for government users, and an internal identity provider for contractor staff. Reconciling these into a coherent model is one of the more persistent architectural problems in the portfolio.

Logging is centralized per program rather than across the division, using a mixture of Splunk and Elastic depending on contract vintage and customer preference. Cross-program visibility is limited and improving it is constrained by data handling rules that in some cases prohibit aggregation.

Our continuous monitoring tooling produces control evidence for roughly 60% of applicable controls automatically. The remainder is collected manually on a quarterly cycle by engineers who would rather be doing something else, which is both a morale problem and a reliability problem.

WHAT WE ARE ACTUALLY LOOKING FOR

Beyond the enumerated qualifications, the characteristics that distinguish successful senior security engineers in this environment are worth stating.

The first is the judgment to distinguish real risk from framework compliance. Our control frameworks are extensive and satisfying every control literally is neither possible nor useful. We need someone who can identify which controls genuinely reduce risk in a given architecture, implement those thoroughly, and handle the remainder through documented and defensible risk acceptance rather than through theater.

The second is the ability to work with engineering teams as a partner rather than as an obstacle. Security engineers who operate primarily by filing findings and escalating are technically correct and organizationally ineffective. The people who succeed here fix things, write the reference implementation, and make the secure path easier than the insecure one.

The third is comfort with the government contracting environment. Decisions take longer than they would elsewhere. Requirements arrive through contractual mechanisms rather than product discussions. Funding is subject to appropriations. Engineers who need a fast-moving environment tend to be unhappy here regardless of how interesting they find the technical work.

SELECTION PROCESS AND TIMELINE

Candidates should be aware that hiring timelines in cleared positions differ substantially from commercial technology hiring. Our interview process itself typically takes three to four weeks, but the interval between offer acceptance and start date depends on clearance verification and, for candidates requiring investigation, can extend considerably.

The process includes a recruiter screen covering clearance status and eligibility, a technical conversation with the hiring manager, a security architecture interview in which the candidate reviews a system design and identifies risks, a code review exercise, a conversation with two program Information System Security Officers who would be frequent collaborators, and a final discussion with the Security Engineering director.

We do not ask puzzle questions. We do ask candidates to reason aloud about tradeoffs, and we are more interested in how a candidate reasons about an unfamiliar system than in whether they arrive at the answer we had in mind.
