NLP Engineer
Cormorant Compliance — Remote (US/UK)

Cormorant builds regulatory change management software for financial institutions. We monitor about 900 regulatory sources across 40 jurisdictions, identify changes that affect a given institution, and map them to that institution's internal policies and controls.

We are hiring an NLP Engineer to work on the document understanding pipeline.

What the pipeline does

Regulatory documents are long, densely cross-referenced, written in legal register, and published in inconsistent formats — some as clean XML, many as PDFs that were scanned from paper in 2009.

Our system has to segment these documents into individually addressable provisions, resolve cross-references (including references to other documents and to superseded versions), classify what each provision requires, and determine whether it applies to a particular institution given its charter, size, and business lines.

Then, when a regulator publishes an amendment, we have to diff it against the prior version at the provision level and tell a compliance officer exactly what changed and what it affects.

Your work

Improve provision-level segmentation and hierarchical structure extraction. This is currently our weakest link and the source of most downstream errors.

Cross-reference resolution, including the genuinely nasty cases: references by description rather than citation, references to provisions that have since been renumbered, and circular references.

Change detection and semantic diffing. A regulator reissuing a document with reformatted whitespace must not generate 400 false change alerts.

Applicability classification, working with our in-house regulatory analysts who provide labels and who will tell you when you are wrong.

Evaluation infrastructure. Our analysts' time is our scarcest resource and we need to spend their labeling effort where it moves the needle.

Requirements

Four or more years of NLP or ML engineering with production experience on document understanding, information extraction, or a related problem.

Strong Python, PyTorch, and the Hugging Face ecosystem.

Experience with long-document processing and the practical problems of documents that exceed model context.

Experience with transformer fine-tuning and with knowing when a fine-tune is the wrong answer versus retrieval or rules.

Comfort with domain experts as collaborators. Our regulatory analysts are not technical and their judgment on what matters is better than yours will be for at least a year.

Nice to have

Legal, regulatory, or financial compliance domain exposure.

Experience with OCR pipelines and the failure modes of scanned documents.

Experience with active learning or weak supervision to make expensive labeling go further.

Experience evaluating LLM approaches against classical NLP baselines. We use LLMs for some tasks and find they lose to a well-tuned classifier for others; we want someone comfortable with that being an empirical question.
